# Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (AI Act) — key provisions, captured verbatim

> Collected **2026-06-23** from the eur-lex OJ text (CELEX `32024R1689`) and the per-article
> reproduction at https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/. Quotes in `"…"` are verbatim from the
> enacted text; blocks marked **[captured summary]** are faithful captures to be re-confirmed
> word-for-word against eur-lex before relying on exact wording. Article/annex/recital **numbers**
> are stable across sources and are what `docs/regulatory/eu-ai-act.md` cites.

## Article 2 — Scope  ·  [captured summary]
Applies to: (a) **providers** placing on the market / putting into service AI systems or GPAI
models in the Union, irrespective of establishment; (b) **deployers** of AI systems that have
their place of establishment or are located **within the Union**; (c) providers and deployers in
**third countries** where the **output** produced by the AI system is **used in the Union**; plus
importers, distributors, and product manufacturers placing a system with their product under their
own name. Exclusions include: military/defence/national-security-only use; systems/models developed
and put into service **for the sole purpose of scientific research**; **research, testing or
development prior to placing on the market**; use by a natural person in a **purely personal
non-professional activity**; and systems released under **free and open-source licences** (unless
high-risk or prohibited).  *(Source: Art. 2(1) and exclusions, eur-lex CELEX 32024R1689.)*

## Article 3 — Definitions (verbatim)
- **3(1) "AI system":** "a machine-based system that is designed to operate with varying levels of
  autonomy and that may exhibit adaptiveness after deployment, and that, for explicit or implicit
  objectives, infers, from the input it receives, how to generate outputs such as predictions,
  content, recommendations, or decisions that can influence physical or virtual environments".
- **3(3) "provider":** "a natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body that
  develops an AI system or a general-purpose AI model or that has an AI system or a general-purpose
  AI model developed and places it on the market or puts the AI system into service under its own
  name or trademark".
- **3(4) "deployer":** "a natural or legal person, public authority, agency or other body using an
  AI system under its authority except where the AI system is used in the course of a personal
  non-professional activity".
- **3(63) "general-purpose AI model":** "an AI model … that displays significant generality and is
  capable of competently performing a wide range of distinct tasks … and that can be integrated
  into a variety of downstream systems or applications".

## Recital 12 — what is NOT an AI system (verbatim fragments)
- The definition "should not cover systems that are based on the rules defined solely by natural
  persons to automatically execute operations".
- "A key characteristic of AI systems is their capability to infer. This capability to infer refers
  to the process of obtaining the outputs, such as predictions, content, recommendations, or
  decisions" — distinguishing them from "simpler traditional software systems or programming
  approaches" and "basic data processing".

## Article 4 — AI literacy (verbatim)
"Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a
sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use
of AI systems on their behalf, taking into account their technical knowledge, experience, education
and training and the context the AI systems are to be used in …". *Applies from 2 Feb 2025
(Art. 113(a), Chapter I).*

## Article 5(1) — Prohibited practices (verbatim fragments)
(a) "subliminal techniques beyond a person's consciousness or purposefully manipulative or
deceptive techniques" causing material behavioural distortion + significant harm; (b) "exploits
any of the vulnerabilities of a natural person or a specific group … due to their age, disability
or a specific social or economic situation"; (c) social scoring — "evaluation or classification of
natural persons … based on their social behaviour or … personal or personality characteristics"
leading to detrimental treatment; (d) predictive policing based "solely on the profiling of a
natural person or on assessing their personality traits"; (e) "untargeted scraping of facial images
from the internet or CCTV footage"; (f) "infer emotions of a natural person in the areas of
workplace and education institutions" (save medical/safety); (g) biometric categorisation deducing
race, political opinions, trade-union membership, religious/philosophical beliefs, sex life or
sexual orientation; (h) "'real-time' remote biometric identification systems in publicly accessible
spaces for the purposes of law enforcement".

## Article 6 — High-risk classification (verbatim fragments)
- **6(1):** AI systems that are a **safety component** of, or are themselves, products covered by
  the Union harmonisation legislation in **Annex I** and require third-party conformity assessment.
- **6(2):** AI systems listed in **Annex III** are high-risk.
- **6(3) derogation:** an Annex III system is **not** high-risk if it "does not pose a significant
  risk of harm to the health, safety or fundamental rights of natural persons", which holds when it
  is intended to (a) "perform a narrow procedural task"; (b) "improve the result of a previously
  completed human activity"; (c) "detect decision-making patterns or deviations … and is not meant
  to replace or influence the previously completed human assessment, without proper human review";
  or (d) "perform a preparatory task to an assessment relevant" to an Annex III use case.
- **6(3) final subparagraph (verbatim):** "Notwithstanding the first subparagraph, an AI system
  referred to in Annex III shall always be considered to be high-risk where the AI system performs
  profiling of natural persons."

## Annex III — High-risk areas (the 8 areas; point 5 verbatim)
Areas: 1 Biometrics · 2 Critical infrastructure · 3 Education & vocational training · 4 Employment /
workers management · **5 Access to and enjoyment of essential private and public services** · 6 Law
enforcement · 7 Migration, asylum & border control · 8 Administration of justice & democratic
processes.
- **5(b) (verbatim):** "AI systems intended to be used to evaluate the creditworthiness of natural
  persons or establish their credit score, **with the exception of AI systems used for the purpose
  of detecting financial fraud**".
- **5(c) (verbatim):** "AI systems intended to be used for risk assessment and pricing in relation
  to natural persons in the case of life and health insurance".

## Article 25(1) — when a downstream party becomes a provider (verbatim fragments)
A distributor/importer/deployer/third party becomes a provider of a high-risk system where: they
"put their name or trademark on a high-risk AI system already placed on the market"; they "make a
substantial modification to a high-risk AI system … in such a way that it remains a high-risk AI
system"; or they modify "the intended purpose of an AI system, including a general-purpose AI
system … in such a way that the AI system concerned becomes a high-risk AI system".

## Article 50 — Transparency (verbatim fragments; provider vs deployer)
- **50(1) provider:** providers ensure AI systems "intended to interact directly with natural
  persons" inform persons that they are interacting with an AI system, unless this is "obvious from
  the point of view of a natural person who is reasonably well-informed".
- **50(2) provider:** providers of AI systems "generating synthetic audio, image, video or text
  content" mark outputs "in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or
  manipulated", subject to technical feasibility/cost; exemptions for assistive/standard-editing
  functions and law enforcement.
- **50(3) deployer:** deployers of **emotion-recognition / biometric-categorisation** systems
  inform exposed persons.
- **50(4) deployer:** deployers of **deep fakes** disclose the content is artificially generated;
  deployers of AI-generated **text published to inform the public on matters of public interest**
  disclose it — with exemptions (editorial review, artistic/satirical, law enforcement).
- **50(5):** the information "shall be provided … in a clear and distinguishable manner at the
  latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure".
*Article 50 applies from 2 Aug 2026 (Art. 113, general application).*

## Article 53 — GPAI model provider obligations (verbatim fragments)
- **53(1)(a):** maintain technical documentation incl. "training and testing process and the
  results of its evaluation" (Annex XI). **53(1)(b):** make available to downstream providers who
  integrate the model the information/documentation enabling them to "have a good understanding of
  the capabilities and limitations" (Annex XII). **53(1)(c):** "a policy to comply with Union law on
  copyright and related rights". **53(1)(d):** "a sufficiently detailed summary about the content
  used for training".
- **53(2) FOSS exemption:** 53(1)(a)–(b) do not apply to models released under "a free and
  open-source licence that allows for the access, usage, modification, and distribution of the
  model", with public parameters — **except** GPAI models with systemic risk.
*Chapter V (GPAI) applies from 2 Aug 2025 (Art. 113(b)).*

## Article 99 — Penalties (verbatim)
- **99(3)** (Art. 5 breach): "up to 35 000 000 EUR or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 7 %
  of its total worldwide annual turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher."
- **99(4)** (other obligations, incl. Art. 50 transparency and provider/deployer duties): "up to
  15 000 000 EUR or, if the offender is an undertaking, up to 3 % of its total worldwide annual
  turnover for the preceding financial year, whichever is higher."
- **99(6)** (SMEs/start-ups): "each fine shall be up to the percentages or amount referred to in
  paragraphs 3, 4 and 5, whichever thereof is lower."

## Article 113 — Entry into force & application (verbatim)
- "This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in
  the Official Journal of the European Union." *(Published 12 Jul 2024 → in force **1 Aug 2024**.)*
- "It shall apply from **2 August 2026**."
- **(a)** "Chapters I and II shall apply from **2 February 2025**." *(Chapter I general provisions
  incl. Art. 4 AI literacy; Chapter II Art. 5 prohibitions.)*
- **(b)** "Chapter III Section 4, Chapter V, Chapter VII and Chapter XII and Article 78 shall apply
  from **2 August 2025**, with the exception of Article 101." *(Notified bodies; GPAI models;
  governance; penalties; confidentiality.)*
- **(c)** "Article 6(1) and the corresponding obligations in this Regulation shall apply from
  **2 August 2027**." *(High-risk via Annex I product safety.)*

## EEA / Norway status  ·  [captured summary — in flux, re-confirm]
The Regulation is **marked EEA-relevant** and is **under scrutiny for incorporation into the EEA
Agreement** by Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway (EFTA EEA-Lex factsheet `32024R1689`). As captured
on 2026-06-23 it had **not yet** been formally incorporated via an EEA Joint Committee decision; the
date it becomes binding in Norwegian law, and the designated Norwegian supervisory authority, follow
that decision and national implementing legislation — **verify against EFTA/Lovdata** before relying
on a Norwegian application date. *(Source: https://www.efta.int/eea-lex/32024r1689.)*
